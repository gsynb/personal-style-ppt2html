# Academic Style Rules

Use these rules when producing serious academic HTML presentations or research notes.

## Tone

- Prefer claim-first headings over decorative slogans.
- Keep visual rhythm quiet: evidence, mechanism, result, implication.
- Use exact terms, units, paper titles, model names, and citation years.
- Preserve bilingual terminology when the source deck mixes Chinese and English.

## Visual System

- Use white or near-white backgrounds unless the reference deck clearly establishes another base.
- Use one accent color for section markers, rules, arrows, and emphasis.
- Avoid decorative gradients, blur, glowing effects, stock-photo hero layouts, and oversized marketing sections.
- Use thin rules, alignment, and whitespace rather than ornamental containers.
- Keep cards rare; use them only for repeated comparable items.

## Slide Anatomy

- Title: short, top-centered or top-left according to the extracted profile.
- Proof object: figure, equation, table, architecture diagram, workflow, or comparison.
- Explanation: short bullets or a concise claim block.
- Citation: small footer when a paper figure, model, or claim is referenced.

## Evidence Discipline

- Never invent experimental values, paper figures, logos, or institution marks.
- Keep generated raster images away from data, charts, model outputs, and factual diagrams.
- Use editable HTML, SVG, Mermaid, MathJax, or chart libraries for scientific diagrams and data views.
